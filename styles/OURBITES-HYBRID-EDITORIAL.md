## Director → motion → Remotion

Before implementation, write episode-local animation-tickets.json using the [ticket contract](../skills/ourbites-creative-director/references/animation-tickets.md). Director owns the observable event and meaning, hero start/end states, framing/depth, contact/occlusion, asset need, narration cue, readable hold and transition rationale. Motion design owns separate spatial paths and temporal curves, anticipation/settle, offsets and velocity at joins. Codex implements those decisions using existing deterministic Remotion components. Viewer reviews actual output against the ticket and prior media. This routing is an agent workflow; executable director invocation/downloads belong to issue43.

Use shallow depth and intentional foreground/midground/background occlusion. Hand/prop contact shares one anchor and transform; camera ownership is explicit. Preserve velocity through a continuous path unless a stop is intentional. Vary hero, camera, type and supporting-object timing according to purpose. A clean cut is valid when it clarifies a new thought; do not manufacture continuous physical action for every transition.

Keep captions and factual caveats in protected screen-space regions. Word fades follow actual narration; phrase-derived timings stay explicitly provisional. Preserve am_puck Voice A at original pitch and 1.1x playback. Keep claims, figures, assets, shot coordinates and timings episode-owned. Use authentic local assets only with appropriate rights evidence; otherwise record an original fallback. Do not invent brand marks, cash implications or full financial reconciliation.

## Fails even when code is correct

- Repeated floating cards or full-frame rectangle growth used as default transitions.
- A decorative magnifier, stationery detail or camera drift hiding a static information board.
- Generic wipes, identical easing on every track, arbitrary bounces or stopping at every path waypoint.
- Props teleporting, hands missing their grip, background and foreground sharing transforms without depth.
- Numerals/headlines replacing the actual mechanism; tiny actors beside long paper-reading holds.
- Removing a stronger existing shot (especially the V2 fire) to make a new draft look different.

## Cumulative review and calibration

Inherit the current candidate/approved base; freeze one recurring limitation and one bounded change hypothesis. Record preserve/improve/replace and compare previous/new media at 1x, phone size, plus adjacent action-boundary frames. Give timestamped observations for opening impact, object action, transitions/framing, reading hierarchy and acting/assets; label technical fix, modest polish or substantial revision. Disclose still-only, missing reference and listening gaps. Tests do not measure taste or retention.

For a **major new direction or replaced weak scene**, propose two different low-cost concepts for the same takeaway (or explain why only one is credible). Select one with a reason and render only that **5–8s, 432×768** proof before expanding the episode. Compare it with the original editorial reel and train/ticket benchmark where accessible. A normal incremental fix inherits the current idea and does not require two new concepts.

Human playback/art acceptance precedes promoting a recipe as visually accepted. Parameterized candidates may demonstrate technical reuse in another business mechanism, but remain provisional. After acceptance, extract only the useful behavior, prove a genuinely different subject, and record reused/configured/bespoke parts. Current motion catalog has no accepted recipes. No merge, CI result or written contract supplies visual, factual, rights or publication approval.
