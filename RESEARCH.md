## Monetization: correct the premise first

YouTube's [expanded YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240?hl=en) distinguishes:

| Route | Subscriber threshold | Activity threshold | What it unlocks |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Earlier access | 500 | 3 public uploads in 90 days, plus 3,000 qualifying long-form hours/12 months OR 3M qualifying Shorts views/90 days | Eligible fan funding/Shopping features |
| Advertising access | 1,000 | 4,000 qualifying long-form hours/12 months OR 10M qualifying Shorts views/90 days | Ads/Premium subject to review, modules and feature rules |

Meeting numbers is not automatic acceptance. Netherlands is listed for expanded YPP. Lifetime views do not satisfy a rolling 90-day threshold. Shorts Feed watch time does not count toward the 4,000-hour long-form route; verify eligibility in Studio's Earn tab using the [YPP eligibility rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en).

**Upcoming change:** the current [Shorts monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en) explicitly dates a monthly revenue eligibility requirement to **1 February 2027**: at least 10M qualifying Shorts views in the trailing 90-day period, measured through the preceding month's 15th. This makes a sustained 1M/month Shorts plan insufficient for that future Creator Pool/Premium threshold. Recheck Studio and the official policy before launch. Some explanatory sections already use present-tense wording; the explicit effective-date paragraph is the basis for the date here. Earlier Shorts views are not paid retroactively.

YouTube changed public Shorts views in March 2025; eligibility and revenue continue to use engaged/qualified views. Do not treat total public starts/replays as eligible views. [Official announcement](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en&msgid=356643701).

If all 140K recent views were eligible Shorts views (unverified), 3M is **21.4x** that level and 10M is **71.4x**. Those are scale comparisons, not growth forecasts. Current pace is roughly 46.7K views/month.

## What $500/month actually requires

Use creator **RPM**, not advertiser CPM. YouTube defines RPM after its share; Shorts use engaged views, while long-form RPM uses views. Actual RPM depends on geography, audience, format and monetization mix. [YouTube metric definitions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314357?hl=en-GB).

All figures below are **sensitivity assumptions**, not researched market averages or a prediction for Ourbites. Revenue means channel receipts before production costs and taxes. Do not subtract YouTube's share again from an RPM figure.

| Assumed creator RPM | Views needed for $500 | Interpretation |
|---:|---:|---|
| $0.05 | 10,000,000 | Low-RPM sensitivity |
| $0.10 | 5,000,000 | Low-RPM sensitivity |
| $0.20 | 2,500,000 | Low-RPM sensitivity |
| $0.50 | 1,000,000 | Justin's implied assumption |
| $2.00 | 250,000 | Long-form planning scenario |
| $5.00 | 100,000 | Long-form planning scenario |
| $10.00 | 50,000 | Long-form planning scenario |

Formula: `required_views = target_revenue / RPM * 1000`. If the target is $500 cash after $100 direct monthly costs, solve for $600 revenue. Unmonetized ad revenue is zero regardless of this table. The future Shorts eligibility threshold is a separate condition; a mathematically sufficient RPM/views combination may still be ineligible.

For the 4,000-hour route, 60,000 qualifying long-form views at a hypothetical four-minute average watch duration produce 4,000 hours. At two minutes, it takes 120,000. This is watch-time arithmetic, not a promise of attainable retention or admission.

## Content format and four visual directions

Editorial promise: **Bite-sized explanations of business.** Explain one business move, how it works and why it matters. Narrow initial beat: tech companies, ownership, pricing power, acquisitions and business models. General world news widens research and accuracy demands while weakening the reason to subscribe.

| Direction | Why it might work | Production tradeoff | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Editorial cutout | Credible, clear visual metaphors, reusable illustrated objects | Separate foreground/background assets; coded camera, arrows and numbers | Recommended first pilot |
| 2. Playful 2D | Memorable narrator character, humor, accessible explanations | Build a small character/pose library; jokes must not replace substance | Runner-up |
| 3. Clay diorama | Distinctive tactile appearance | Multi-shot consistency and iteration cost are unmeasured; reusable 3D assets preferable at scale | Test one clip only |
| 4. Financial comic | Strong energy and contrast | Can become sensational; demands composition variation and restraint | Optional series accent |

These are **style concepts**, not evidence of a currently winning trend. No reference video was supplied, and no controlled audience test has been run. Static images do not establish animation quality, character consistency or retention. The first two are easiest to approximate with layered art and deterministic motion. Exact captions, charts, tickers and numbers should be code-rendered in every style.

The style frames were generated with the built-in image tool; prompt set and files are in `styles/`. They retain the superseded Business & Chats label and are visual references only; all new assets should use Ourbites. The Remotion sample is a separate functional diagram storyboard, not a finished implementation of one of these illustration styles.

## Why AI automation can still fail

The core risk is interchangeable, low-value output. YouTube's [monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) permits materially varied, original value, but rejects generic/repetitive mass-produced work; reused-content review applies to the channel. A common visual system is compatible with varied substance, but it does not guarantee approval. Rewriting a news article over stock imagery is not a defensible editorial product.

Check [synthetic-media disclosure rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en) per episode. Clearly stylized illustrations and production assistance differ from realistic invented events or a real person appearing to say something they did not. Disclosure alone is not a monetization ban. Maintain the factual distinction even when a scene is obviously animated.

## 30-day test and stop/go rules

1. Days 1–3: export Studio metrics; inventory top 20 recent traffic drivers and old clip content. Choose one style and narrator. Produce one factual pilot before expanding code.
2. Week 1: publish two manually approved Shorts; test one small production offer with existing contacts. Do not auto-publish.
3. Weeks 2–4: total target **8 Shorts + 2 long-form explainers**, reducing scope if quality or the 8-hour weekly cap breaks. Each Short must work independently; companion long-form adds explanation instead of stretching it.
4. Measure at 24 hours and seven days. Shorts: engaged views, viewed vs swiped away, average view duration/percentage, subscriber conversion and returning viewers. Long-form: impressions/CTR, retention, qualifying watch time, returning viewers. Compare by format and video age, not lifetime viral totals.
5. Operational gate: target a median repeat Short production time of 90 minutes or less by episode 6, zero unresolved factual corrections and a manageable cost per accepted episode. These are pilot targets, not measured performance.
6. Continue if matched-age results improve against a recorded relevant baseline, viewers respond to the explanation, and cost/time are sustainable. With only eight Shorts, avoid causal certainty. If no comparable recent baseline exists, use the first batch as baseline and run a second fixed batch before scaling.
7. Commercial gate: a paying client validates the service route separately. No sale after 10 well-chosen approaches/three conversations means revise the offer or buyer hypothesis; it does not prove the channel cannot work. Stop increasing spend while both audience and paid-demand signals are absent.
