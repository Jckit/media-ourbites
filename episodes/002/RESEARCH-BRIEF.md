## Central question and editorial decision

**Candidate hook:** "Anthropic disclosed an $80.7 billion net loss in six months. So why was its operating loss only $693 million?" This is a **financial statement / accounting puzzle**, not evidence that Anthropic literally spent $80.7B of cash on operations. Secondary thread: how did its revenue grow so quickly and what financing/compute obligations make further growth complicated?

**What this teaches:** operating versus non-operating gains/losses, fair-value changes on financing instruments, cash versus accounting results, infrastructure economics, and why a large IPO prospectus must be read beyond headline numbers. Balance the extraordinary growth with actual constraints rather than selling a one-directional story. The Short should focus on *one* puzzle; companion longer video may cover operating model, competition, capital commitments and governance separately.

| FY 2024 | 386 | (2,980) | (8,309) |
| FY 2025 | 4,585 | (8,060) | (41,965) |
| Six months ended June 30, 2026 | 16,286 | (693) | (80,748) |

The same six-month 2026 statement shows **$(72,388) million** change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, and **$(8,757) million** other expense, net. The **$(80,748) million net loss** also reflects interest income and taxes. Do not claim the $72,388m component alone explains **all** of the gap. The company reports the fair-value charge was **primarily driven by changes in convertible note values as its enterprise valuation rose**; present that as its disclosed accounting treatment and explain carefully. Do not call reported loss cash burn, and do not compare a six-month period to a full year without a prominent label. Source: S-1 financial table and accompanying notes.

## News accuracy checklist

- Confirm the *latest* registration/approval/pricing/listing status on publication day.
- Avoid "Anthropic IPO happened" wording; distinguish confidential submission, public S-1 filing, SEC effectiveness, pricing and first trading.
- Keep "possible valuation" estimates clearly separate from officially priced offering figures.
- Verify historical funding rounds, customer numbers, compute contracts and governance **from the filing before quoting exact values**. Revenue is not cash flow; adjusted income is not GAAP operating income.
- Recalculate each displayed comparison and identify adjustments/noncash treatment with an appropriate accounting reviewer if conclusions depend on it.

## Short outline (provisional, not yet timed against narration)

1. **Hook:** $80.7B reported net loss vs $693M operating loss; both are six-month results; visual labels stay explicit.
2. **Why this is news:** the company publicly filed the S-1 for a **proposed** IPO; explain that an S-1 exposes detailed financials.
3. **The breakdown:** company sold $16.286B of services in H1, incurred operating loss of $693M, then took material financing fair-value and other non-operating charges.
4. **The complication:** this doesn't mean costs or business risk are unimportant; infrastructure commitments, cash flow, and accounting are separate and need their own evidence.
5. **Payoff:** the biggest number in an IPO headline may not be the best measure of the underlying business's operating performance.
