# Composition and reading priority

Adapted from HyperFrames look guidance; Ourbites brand and existing safe zones remain authoritative.

Specify one dominant subject and the order in which the viewer reads the shot. Define concrete size, crop, contrast and spacing using existing tokens; do not create a parallel palette. Reference footage supplies a visual grammar, not rights to copy its graphics or claims. Separate measured observations from inferred timing.

Web layout is not video layout: small details, thin lines and low-opacity accents may disappear in a phone export. Scale typography and spacing for the delivery frame. Keep claim text, caveats and captions clear of the moving object's full bounds throughout its path and final hold. Give a landing a reserved area before designing its flourish. A beautifully eased object that covers the result is a failed shot.

For typography, choose emphasis that expresses meaning; arrival, readable hold and exit are distinct states. Once landed, stop drift/pulsing unless the explanation requires it. Determine reading time from actual words, narration and phone playback, not a fixed words-per-second rule. Never animate exact values into intermediate unsupported claims.

Inspect the encoded MP4, not only source-resolution stills: H.264 can expose gradient banding and remove weak accents. Reuse the current render settings first; do not add texture or costly effects to hide an unverified problem. Test transparent overlays over representative footage when they are actually requested.

Use the existing Creative Director shot/ticket and viewer acceptance record. No additional SPEC file, design system, template catalog or general animation engine is needed.
