# Competitive formats and retention hypotheses

Research date: 2026-10-27. **Scope:** reference channels and production patterns for Ourbites, not a claim that competitors use a particular AI model or have publicly observable retention. This report extends [RESEARCH.md](../RESEARCH.md) and informs [SOP.md](../SOP.md). Evidence links appear alongside each observation.

## Editorial position

Ourbites = **the business behind the headline**. Use current business news as a timely entry point, then explain the underlying mechanism, incentives, risks, and what changes next. Deliver a useful explanation even after the headline ages. Avoid daily headline recaps, stock-price predictions, and synthetic drama.

The reusable engine may eventually support other channels. Keep business-news tone, examples, source lists, character and visual identity channel-specific; keep evidence tracking, asset provenance, rendering, approval and measurement reusable across channels. Don't split the current single-channel codebase prematurely.

## Story patterns: selectable, not mandatory

1. **Contradiction** — The headline seems to imply X, but evidence suggests Y; show why both can be true. E.g. very large *net* loss versus much smaller *operating* loss.
2. **Business mechanism** — A real decision (pricing, merger, IPO, financing, contract) reveals a repeatable concept. Explain the economics with concrete numbers where sourced.
3. **Strategic reversal** — What changed in the incentives or constraints that undermined a formerly strong strategy? Avoid claiming one action caused a result without evidence.
4. **Power / ownership** — Who controls what? Distinguish ownership, voting power, financial exposure and governance.
5. **Decision under uncertainty** — What do we know now; what is conjecture; which future evidence will settle the question?
6. **Business origin and evolution** — How a product gained customers, monetized, scaled, and encountered constraints, with sourced timeline and real events.

Preferred sequence for Shorts (not a fixed formula):
**Concrete hook / consequence → minimum necessary context → mechanism with a real example → strongest complication or counterpoint → payoff: what to understand differently.**
At least one new, useful piece of information or visual relationship should appear in each meaningful beat. Do not add artificial cliffhangers or cuts just to simulate pacing. Long-form can include origin, competing hypotheses, evidence, and uncertainty in greater depth.

## Hypotheses to test, not results

- A specific, answerable contradiction may earn more *stayed-to-watch* than a generic breaking-news headline.
- Genuine footage, official screens and primary-document excerpts may make a timely business story more concrete than continuous generated B-roll; the value and licensing cost must be measured.
- A single visually precise financial mechanism may outperform multiple disconnected facts.
- A playful, restrained narrator may distinguish Ourbites from a dry recap without reducing trust.
- One evidence packet can underpin a Short, long-form companion and later update, **provided each video has a distinct value proposition**.

## Video-level benchmark protocol

For every chosen competitor video capture: channel, watch URL, title, publish date, observed-at date, duration, views-at-observation, whether current-event or evergreen, opening spoken/visual hook (brief *paraphrase*, not copied script), initial stakes, timeline of narrative beats, turning point, claim quality, visuals used (real footage / archival photos / documents / screenshots / charts / generative visuals), audio and transition behavior, close/payoff, and an inference-confidence label. Annotate 0–5s, 5–15s, 15–30s, and subsequent key beats for Shorts; use structural beats for long-form.

Do **not** reverse-engineer actual competitor retention from public view counts. For Ourbites, collect [YouTube Studio's video-level retention report](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415) and Shorts stayed-to-watch, engaged views, average percentage viewed, and subscriber conversions alongside production hours and copyright outcomes. Compare **same-length, similar-age** tests; retain hypotheses even when disproven.
